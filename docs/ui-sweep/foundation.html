Your testers aren't bouncing off the looks. They're bouncing off the shape.

Four rooms of instruments, opened onto a hardware tour, written in 9-pixel jargon. Nothing here says what's wrong with my PC — which is the only sentence any of your friends came in with. Below: what the apps non-technical people actually like do instead, and a front door for Compy built on it.

01 · What we already measured

The overwhelm is quantifiable

Taken off your installed build (3155c7a0) with a debug port, not guessed from screenshots.

57%of all text on one screen renders at 11 pixels or smaller. Your users are gamers on 1080p screens, often sitting back from them.
12distinct font sizes on a single screen. A scale with twelve steps isn't a hierarchy — it's noise, and the eye can't find the important thing.
1element on the whole page is larger than 19px — and it's the word “Home”, which tells the user nothing.
10steps of hardware tour before a first-time user is allowed to reach the thing they opened the app to find out.

Contrast is already fixed — 32 pieces of unreadable text went to zero last train. The four above are structural, and no repaint touches them.

02 · Research

What every app your friends already use has in common

These aren't picked for beauty. They're picked because non-technical people operate them without help, and they all solve the same problem Compy has: an expert subject, an amateur audience.

Windows SecurityMicrosoft · the closest analogue
One green shield and the sentence “No action needed.” Every detail is one click down. Steal: the front door is a verdict, not a dashboard. Your users already know this app — matching it buys instant familiarity.
TurboTaxIntuit · the interview model
Takes the single most intimidating task in American adult life and asks one plain question per screen, hiding every question that doesn't apply to you. Steal: guide with questions, not with controls.
Battery HealthApple · honest limits
A percentage, one sentence of what it means, one recommendation. It says “we can't know X” rather than inventing X. Steal: your honesty rules are a design asset — say the unknown in plain words instead of hiding it in a tooltip.
Google Security Checkupthe guided sweep
Runs itself, then presents findings as a short ranked list where each row is a plain sentence plus one button. Steal: findings are sentences with a button, never rows in a table.
Duolingoone thing per screen
Never shows two decisions at once, and every screen has exactly one obvious next tap. Steal: if a screen has more than one primary button, it has none.

The principle underneath all five is progressive disclosure: show the answer first, the reasoning on request, the instruments on demand. Compy currently ships in reverse order.

05 · The foundation

Colour: four meanings, one accent, never colour alone

Your own token file states the law — “colour is information, never decoration” — and the app breaks it: the Lab's green is a hair off the “this is good” green, and the Blackbook's amber is a hair off the “needs attention” amber. So the same colour means two different things depending on which room you're in. That is the definition of confusing.

Healthy Nothing to do. Only ever this.#3ddc97
Worth fixing Safe to ignore, better not to.#f5c542

Rooms stop owning a colour. Every green in the app means healthy; every amber means worth fixing. This is a deletion, not a repaint — and it's the single change that does most for comprehension. Roughly one man in twelve can't separate your Lab green from your status green anyway, which is why every chip above carries a shape and a word, not just a hue.

06 · Your question

What I actually need to do this

Honest answer first: no tool was the blocker for anything above. I measured the app, found the problems and drew the fix with what's already wired. Tools make the next part — building and proving it across a whole app — faster and safer. Ranked by what they'd actually change.

1
You · one setting Turn subagents back on for this work

They're switched off in this session. A redesign wants three or four layout directions explored side by side and judged, plus the consumer-panel personas reacting to each. Serial, that's days; parallel, it's an afternoon. This is the single biggest limiter.

2
You · one account decision Figma, plus its MCP server

Lets me read real design files — components, spacing, colour variables — and build directly from them, and lets you point at a layout you like and have me pull it apart. Free tier is enough. Setup is claude mcp add once you've enabled Dev Mode. Without it I'm designing in HTML mockups like this one, which works but can't be handed to anyone else.

3
Me · free, no permission needed An accessibility engine in the test suite

axe-core plus the contrast/type measurement I already wrote, run on every build. Turns “is this readable” from an argument into a failing test. Your bug→guard law says this is how a fix counts as done.

4
Me · free The shadcn component MCP

A library of keyboard-accessible, screen-reader-correct dialogs, menus and form controls. Not for the look — for the plumbing underneath, so I spend the effort on Compy's voice instead of rebuilding a dropdown.

5
Already wired · costs credits v0

Generates React layouts fast. Good for throwing five versions of a screen at the wall. Every call spends your credits, so I'd use it for exploration only and hand-build what ships.

6
You · costs nothing but time Thirty minutes with two of those friends

The one thing no tool replaces. Sit them in front of it, say nothing, and ask them to find out why their game stutters. Where they stop is the real backlog. Record it if you can — I'll turn it into fixes. Everything above is my judgement; this is evidence.

07 · If you say go

The order I'd build it

01
Type and colour, app-wide

Six sizes, 13px floor, kill the Arial fallthrough, rooms give up their hue. Mechanical, low-risk, and it makes every screen in the app better at once. About a day.

02
The front door

Verdict becomes the headline, counters become footer, first run lands here instead of on the hardware tour. This is the change your friends will feel within two seconds.

03
The interview

Promote “what are you here to do?” into a real guided flow with the plain-English finding card. The biggest build, and the one that makes Compy feel like a doctor instead of a dashboard.

04
Rewrite the words

The glossary above, applied everywhere, with a test that fails the build if a banned term comes back.

05
The instruments stay — behind a door

Monitor, Lab and the record aren't deleted. They stop being the first thing anyone sees. Your power users lose nothing; your friends stop drowning.
