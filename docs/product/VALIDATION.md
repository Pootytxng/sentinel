# Product Validation

**Derived from:** `../PRODUCT_CONSTITUTION.md`

Compy is technically advanced but market value is not proven by engineering completeness. Product development must collect external evidence early enough to challenge the roadmap.

## 1. What must be validated

Four different claims require different evidence:

1. **Usability** — normal users can reach value without owner coaching.
2. **Technical usefulness** — Compy produces materially useful evidence/actions on real machines.
3. **Demand** — target users care enough to install, return, share, or join a waitlist.
4. **Willingness to pay** — activated users value specific paid capabilities at real prices.

Do not use one as a substitute for another.

## 2. Real-case requirement

Synthetic fixtures and internal rigs prove correctness, not product usefulness.

A meaningful beta must include real cases such as:

- unexplained stutter;
- crash/freeze regression;
- questionable optimization-guide damage;
- driver/update regression;
- CPU/GPU bottleneck uncertainty;
- performance optimization on varied hardware;
- upgrade uncertainty.

## 3. Recommended decision thresholds

These are internal management thresholds, not universal market benchmarks.

Before claiming the Care thesis is working:

- collect at least 10 genuine problem cases across multiple PCs;
- aim for Compy to materially narrow the problem or provide a clearly useful next action in roughly 7/10 or better;
- investigate every failure mode rather than averaging it away.

Before claiming the guided-performance thesis is working:

- test across materially different CPU/GPU/platform classes;
- demonstrate that candidate selection changes appropriately by machine/workload;
- demonstrate at least some honest null/worse results and correct restoration;
- confirm first-time users can complete the guided process without external benchmarking knowledge.

## 4. Time-to-first-value

Target: a qualified first-time user should reach a useful evidence-backed answer quickly enough that the product feels easier than searching forums/videos.

A practical initial target is that a majority of users can reach first useful evidence within about ten minutes, excluding unavoidable long benchmark/stability runs.

## 5. Demand tests

Run tests with qualified traffic rather than broad low-intent visitors.

Measure:

- landing-page signup/install intent;
- install completion;
- first useful result;
- return after a new problem/update/game;
- receipt/report sharing;
- recommendation to another PC user.

Do not treat raw page views as proof of demand.

## 6. Willingness to pay

Do not arm a monetization stack merely because pricing research exists.

After users receive real value, present realistic offers and measure:

- click intent;
- checkout start;
- price sensitivity;
- which capabilities users believe deserve Pro;
- whether one-time, annual, maintenance, or subscription structure matches perceived value.

## 7. Competitive testing

Compy should be compared by outcome and workflow, not tweak count.

Useful comparisons include:

- time to understand what to do;
- amount of technical knowledge required;
- ability to explain applicability to this machine;
- rollback quality;
- benchmark rigor;
- ability to show null results;
- diagnosis after a regression;
- quality of final user decision.

## 8. Stop / rethink conditions

Substantially rethink the product or target if, after a fair external test:

- qualified users still cannot understand the product without coaching;
- Care rarely produces anything more useful than built-in/free alternatives;
- per-rig optimization cannot produce more trustworthy decisions than static presets;
- users consider receipts/rollback interesting but not valuable;
- qualified traffic shows persistently negligible activation;
- activated users show essentially no willingness to pay for any proposed durable value.

## 9. Strong continue signals

Increase investment when users repeatedly express outcomes equivalent to:

> Compy figured out in minutes what I was trying to solve for hours.

or:

> It tested the settings I would never have known how to compare myself.

or:

> It stopped me from buying/changing something that would not have solved the problem.

## 10. Evidence discipline

Every roadmap expansion should name the product hypothesis it is intended to validate.

If no observable user or technical result can falsify the hypothesis, the work is not ready to outrank validated needs.
